Thierry Lahaye is Directeur de recherche (Senior Researcher, DR2) at the French National Centre for Scientific Research (CNRS), based at the Laboratoire Charles Fabry, Institut d'Optique Graduate School in Palaiseau, France. He co-leads the "Quantum Optics – Atoms" group with Antoine Browaeys, where the team pioneers neutral-atom quantum simulation and computing using arrays of single Rydberg atoms in optical tweezers.

He completed his PhD in 2006 at the Laboratoire Kastler-Brossel, École Normale Supérieure, Paris, under the supervision of Jean Dalibard, studying evaporative cooling of magnetically guided atomic beams. He then carried out postdoctoral research (2006–2008) in Tilman Pfau's group at Universität Stuttgart, where he was a key contributor to realising the first strongly dipolar and then purely dipolar Bose-Einstein condensate using chromium atoms — landmark results published in Nature and Nature Physics. He held CNRS researcher positions in Toulouse (2008–2011) and then at Institut d'Optique, Palaiseau (2012–present), being promoted to DR2 in 2022. He defended his Habilitation à diriger des recherches (HDR) at Institut d'Optique in May 2015.

At Institut d'Optique, Lahaye co-leads work on programmable Rydberg atom arrays, including defect-free atom assembly, large-scale quantum Ising simulations, and programmable quantum simulation of frustrated magnets with hundreds of atoms. In 2019, he co-founded Pasqal, the quantum computing company commercialising neutral-atom processors based on this group's technology.
