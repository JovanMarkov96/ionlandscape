Hugo Zbinden (born 1961, Bern) is Honorary Professor of Physics at the University of Geneva, where he spent more than three decades at the Department of Applied Physics. He studied physics at the University of Bern, receiving an MSc in climate physics (1987) and a PhD in laser physics (1991). After a short postdoctoral industry project in Bern, he joined the University of Geneva in 1993 as a postdoc in Nicolas Gisin's group, was appointed MER (Maître d'enseignement et de recherche) in 1998, promoted to Associate Professor in 2012 when he launched the dedicated Quantum Technologies group, and retired in 2023 while retaining an honorary professorship. He is currently also involved in establishing the Vigo Quantum Communication Center at the University of Vigo.

Zbinden is a pioneer of practical quantum key distribution in optical fibres and co-inventor of the plug-and-play QKD system, which eliminated many sources of systematic error in fibre-based QKD. His early work with Gisin, Tittel, and Brendel in the late 1990s established the feasibility of distributing energy-time entangled photon pairs and violating Bell inequalities over deployed telecom fibre (10 km, 1998). The group's 2002 Reviews of Modern Physics article on quantum cryptography (with Gisin, Ribordy, and Tittel) has accumulated more than 12,000 citations and remains the canonical reference in the field. In 2003, a Nature paper demonstrated long-distance teleportation of qubits at telecom wavelengths. A central theme of Zbinden's later career was advancing superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors (SNSPDs) for telecom wavelengths, enabling a 2018 Physical Review Letters paper reporting secure QKD over a record 421 km of optical fibre. He is co-author of approximately 200 scientific publications and co-founder of ID Quantique (founded 2001 with Nicolas Gisin and Grégoire Ribordy), one of the first commercial quantum-technology companies.
