Hannes Pichler is a theoretical physicist and Professor of Theoretical Physics at the University of Innsbruck, where he has held his chair since June 2020. He is also Research Director of the Pichler Group — Quantum Science Theory at the Institute for Quantum Optics and Quantum Information (IQOQI) of the Austrian Academy of Sciences in Innsbruck. He was born in 1986 in Brixen (South Tyrol, Italy).

He received his PhD in 2015 from the University of Innsbruck in the research group of Peter Zoller, working on quantum simulators, entanglement detection, and open many-body dynamics. He subsequently held postdoctoral fellowships at Harvard University as an ITAMP Fellow (2016–2019) and at the California Institute of Technology as a Gordon and Betty Moore Fellow (2019–2020).

Pichler's research centers on theoretical quantum optics, quantum information, and quantum many-body physics. He is a leading theorist on the use of Rydberg atom arrays for programmable quantum simulation and quantum computing, and has made foundational contributions to photonic quantum systems and waveguide QED. He co-authored the landmark 51-atom Rydberg quantum simulator paper (Nature 2017) and contributed to the 256-atom simulator (Nature 2021) and topological spin-liquid experiments (Science 2021), all from the Lukin group at Harvard. Since returning to Innsbruck his group has developed new theoretical frameworks for universal quantum computation in globally driven Rydberg arrays and fermionic quantum processing with neutral atoms.

He has received numerous awards including the New Horizons in Physics Prize (2023), an ERC Starting Grant (2022), the Hans and Walter Thirring Prize of the Austrian Academy of Sciences (2023), the Ignaz L. Lieben Prize (2024), and has been listed as a Highly Cited Researcher in physics (2023–2025).
