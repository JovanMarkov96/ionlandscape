Andrew J. Daley is Professor of Quantum Physics in the Atomic and Laser Physics sub-department at the University of Oxford, and Tutorial Fellow in Physics at Keble College. His research spans the boundary between quantum optics, many-body physics, and quantum technologies, focusing on non-equilibrium dynamics of ultracold quantum gases, open quantum systems, and tensor-network numerical methods.

He received his PhD in 2005 from the University of Innsbruck under the supervision of Peter Zoller, with a thesis titled "Manipulation and Simulation of Cold Atoms in Optical Lattices." He subsequently held postdoctoral positions in Innsbruck before joining the University of Pittsburgh as Assistant Professor in 2011. He was appointed full Professor at the University of Strathclyde in 2013, where he led the Quantum Optics and Quantum Many-body Systems (QOQMS) group in the Department of Physics, before moving to Oxford.

Daley co-developed the time-dependent DMRG (t-DMRG) method with adaptive Hilbert spaces (2004), which became a standard tool for simulating quantum dynamics in 1D systems. He proposed quantum computing schemes with alkaline-earth-metal atoms (2008) and group-II atoms (2011), and has made foundational contributions to the theory of open many-body quantum systems via quantum trajectories (2014). His 2022 Nature review article on practical quantum advantage in quantum simulation is widely cited. He is a Fellow of the American Physical Society (elected 2021) and leads the UK National Research Hub for Quantum Computing via Integrated and Interconnected Implementations (QCI3).
