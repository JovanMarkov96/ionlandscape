Luming Duan is the Yao Qizhi Chair Professor at Tsinghua University's Institute for Interdisciplinary Information Sciences (IIIS), an Academician of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (2023), and a Principal Investigator at the Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute. 

Before returning full-time to Tsinghua in 2018, he had a distinguished career in the US, joining the University of Michigan in 2003 and eventually holding the Fermi Collegiate Professor chair. He received his B.S. and Ph.D. (1998) from the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC) under Guo Guangcan, and later conducted postdoctoral research at the University of Innsbruck.

His research bridges profound theoretical insights with groundbreaking experimental achievements. He is globally renowned as the "D" in the DLCZ (Duan-Lukin-Cirac-Zoller) protocol for long-distance quantum communication. More recently, his experimental group at Tsinghua has achieved major breakthroughs in trapped-ion quantum simulation, including the stable trapping of up to 512 ions in a 2D crystal and performing quantum simulations with 300 ion qubits—representing the largest-scale multi-ion quantum simulation with single-qubit resolution to date.
