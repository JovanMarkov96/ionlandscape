Hartmut Häffner is a professor of physics at UC Berkeley and affiliated with Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, leading The Ion Trap Group focused on trapped-ion quantum science.

He received his PhD from the University of Mainz in 2000 under Günter Werth. After short postdoctoral periods in Mainz and Bangalore, he received a Feodor-Lynen fellowship and went to NIST Gaithersburg (2000–2001). In 2001 he moved to the University of Innsbruck, where he held a Marie-Curie fellowship (2002–2004) and worked as senior scientist at IQOQI (2004–2009), winning the Austrian START award in 2006. He joined UC Berkeley as Assistant Professor in 2009.

His research spans quantum information processing, quantum simulation with ion crystals in RF and optical potentials, scalable ion-trap architectures (segmented and planar traps), and novel ion–solid-state coupling experiments. He is also affiliated with the Berkeley Center for Integrated Quantum Information and Computation (CIQC).

## Key Publications
- "Quantum computing with trapped ions," H. Häffner, C.F. Roos, R. Blatt, Physics Reports 469, 155 (2008).
- "Scalable multiparticle entanglement of trapped ions," H. Häffner et al., Nature 438, 643 (2005).
- "Deterministic quantum teleportation with atoms," M. Riebe, H. Häffner et al., Nature 429, 734 (2004).
