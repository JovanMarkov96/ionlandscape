Ulrik Lund Andersen (born 1972) is Professor of Physics at the Technical University of Denmark (DTU) and Director of bigQ — the Center for Macroscopic Quantum States — where he heads the Quantum Information group. He is one of the leading figures in continuous-variable (CV) quantum information, pioneering experimental and theoretical work on squeezed light, photonic cluster states, CV quantum key distribution, hybrid discrete-CV protocols, and bosonic error correction with the Gottesman–Kitaev–Preskill (GKP) code.

Andersen received both his MSc (1999) and PhD (2003) in Applied Physics from DTU, where his thesis work on optical squeezing involved collaboration with Preben Buchhave. From 2003 to 2006 he was a postdoc and assistant professor in Gerd Leuchs's group at the University of Erlangen-Nuremberg (Institut für Optik, Information und Photonik), publishing key results on quantum cloning and polarization-state manipulation with linear optics. He returned to DTU as Associate Professor in 2006 and was promoted to full Professor and section leader in 2012. He became founding Director of bigQ in January 2018.

His landmark 2019 Science paper (DOI: 10.1126/science.aay4354) demonstrated the deterministic generation of a two-dimensional photonic cluster state with more than 30,000 entangled modes — a scalable resource for measurement-based quantum computation. In 2025, the same group reported the first proven quantum advantage on a photonic platform, achieving an 11.8-orders-of-magnitude reduction in sample complexity for a quantum learning task (Science, DOI: 10.1126/science.adv2560). His 2015 Nature Physics review (DOI: 10.1038/nphys3410) on hybrid discrete- and continuous-variable quantum information has become a widely cited conceptual framework. He is also a co-author on the landmark 2020 Advances in Optics and Photonics review "Advances in quantum cryptography" (>2,800 citations).

Awards include the Danish EliteForsk Prize (2013) and three Sapere Aude awards from the Danish Council for Independent Research (Young Researcher 2008, Scientific Leader 2011, Top Researcher 2015). Research funding includes the Danish National Research Foundation (DNRF142, bigQ) and the European Research Council (ERC-CoG FRECOM, grant 771878).

He has co-founded two DTU spin-outs: Alea Quantum Technologies (2022), which is commercialising a chip-integrated quantum random number generator based on vacuum fluctuation measurements for mobile-phone encryption; and DIASENSE (2024), which develops quantum diamond magnetic microscopes for semiconductor failure analysis.
