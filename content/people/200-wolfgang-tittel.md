Wolfgang Tittel (born c. 1969, Germany) is Professor and Chair in Quantum Communications at the University of Geneva, holding a joint appointment with Constructor University established in January 2023. He is one of the leading experimental physicists in quantum communication over optical fibre, with foundational contributions to long-distance quantum entanglement distribution, quantum key distribution, quantum teleportation, and solid-state quantum memories for quantum repeaters.

Tittel completed his PhD in 2000 at the University of Geneva under Nicolas Gisin (182), where he was a central member of the Geneva quantum optics group alongside Hugo Zbinden (187) and Grégoire Ribordy. His doctoral work contributed directly to the landmark 1998 Physical Review Letters paper on violation of Bell inequalities by photons more than 10 km apart in deployed telecom fibre — one of the first demonstrations that long-distance quantum correlations survive in real-world infrastructure. The group's 2002 Reviews of Modern Physics survey, "Quantum cryptography" (Gisin, Ribordy, Tittel, Zbinden), has accumulated more than 12,000 citations and remains the canonical reference in the field.

After postdoctoral work with Eugene Polzik in Aarhus on photon echoes and quantum memory, Tittel joined the University of Calgary in 2006 as associate professor and AITF Industrial Research Chair in Quantum Secured Communication, becoming full professor in 2013. His Calgary group pioneered the use of rare-earth-ion-doped crystals — in particular Eu:Y₂SiO₅ and Tm-doped waveguides — as multimode optical quantum memories operating at telecom wavelengths, using the atomic frequency comb (AFC) protocol. In 2016 his group demonstrated quantum teleportation across the Calgary metropolitan fibre network, teleporting photons over 6 km of deployed fibre. In 2018 he moved to QuTech at Delft University of Technology to work on the quantum internet before returning to Geneva in 2023. He is a founding member of the QCRYPT conference series, a founding editor of the IOP journal Quantum Science and Technology, and a member of the Swiss Quantum Commission.
