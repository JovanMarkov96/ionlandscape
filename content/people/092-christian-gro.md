Christian Groß is W3 Professor of Experimental Many-Particle Quantum Physics at the University of Tübingen, where he has led the Quantum Many-Body Physics group since September 2019. He is one of the foremost experimentalists in quantum gas microscopy, having co-developed a world-leading platform using ultracold fermionic lithium atoms in optical lattices that enables single-atom and single-spin resolved imaging of strongly correlated quantum many-body systems.

He obtained his PhD summa cum laude from Ruprecht-Karls-Universität Heidelberg in 2010 under Prof. Markus Oberthaler, with a thesis on spin squeezing and non-linear atom interferometry with Bose–Einstein condensates. He then joined Immanuel Bloch's Quantum Many-Body Systems Division at the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics (MPQ) as a project leader (2011), becoming research group leader there in 2017 before his Tübingen appointment. His MPQ work in collaboration with Bloch produced landmark demonstrations of antiferromagnetic correlations, spin-charge separation, and magnetic polarons in Fermi–Hubbard chains via quantum gas microscopy.

He received the DPG Dissertation Prize (2011), an ERC Starting Grant (2015), the Heinz Maier-Leibnitz Prize (2017), a DFG Heisenberg Professorship (2018), and the Alfried Krupp Prize for Young University Teachers (2019). His Tübingen group is developing a cryogenic strontium-87 quantum processor platform as part of the MUNIQC-Atoms consortium, and separately exploring Rydberg-dressed quantum many-body systems.
