Dr. Serge Rosenblum is an experimental physicist at the Weizmann Institute of Science, where his group builds superconducting quantum circuits for quantum information processing. His research centers on circuit QED and error-correctable **bosonic qubits** encoded in long-lived superconducting microwave cavities, aiming at hardware-efficient quantum error correction.

Rosenblum completed his PhD at the Weizmann Institute under Prof. Barak Dayan, working on single-atom quantum optics — including an all-optical single-photon switch controlled by a single photon and the deterministic extraction of a single photon from an optical pulse. He then carried out postdoctoral research at Yale University in the group of Prof. Robert Schoelkopf, where he developed error-correctable qubits stored in microwave cavities and demonstrated a CNOT gate between photonic qubits encoded in two cavities. He returned to the Weizmann Institute to establish his own laboratory, where recent work has produced superconducting cavity qubits with single-photon coherence times of tens of milliseconds.
