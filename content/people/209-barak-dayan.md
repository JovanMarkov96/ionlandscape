Prof. Barak Dayan is an experimental physicist in the Department of Chemical and Biological Physics at the Weizmann Institute of Science, where he leads the **Quantum Optics group**. His research centers on achieving deterministic photon-photon interactions at the single-photon level — using laser-cooled atoms coupled to optical microresonators to let a single atom, or even a single photon, control another photon. His group demonstrated a single-atom all-optical switch and a passive photon-atom quantum gate, foundational primitives for photonic quantum information.

Dayan earned his PhD at the Weizmann Institute under Prof. Yaron Silberberg, working on nonlinear and quantum optics with broadband down-converted light. He then carried out postdoctoral research at the California Institute of Technology in the group of Prof. H. Jeff Kimble, where he demonstrated a "photon turnstile" — a single atom coupled to a microtoroidal resonator regulating photons one at a time (*Science*, 2008). He returned to the Weizmann Institute to establish his own laboratory and received the Krill Prize in 2015.

He is also Chief Scientist and Co-Founder of **Quantum Source Labs**, an Israeli company building scalable photonic quantum computers around deterministic photon-atom interactions.
