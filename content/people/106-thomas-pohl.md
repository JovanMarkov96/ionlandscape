Thomas Pohl is a Professor of Theoretical Physics at the Institute for Theoretical Physics at TU Wien (Vienna University of Technology), where he leads the Pohl Group focusing on many-body quantum optics and light-matter interactions. He previously held a Niels Bohr Professorship at Aarhus University (2017–2023) funded by the Danish National Research Foundation, and was a Group Leader at the Max Planck Institute for the Physics of Complex Systems in Dresden (2008–2017).

He received his PhD in Physics in 2005 from the Max Planck Institute for the Physics of Complex Systems in Dresden, where he worked in the Finite Systems division under Jan-Michael Rost. After his doctorate he held an ITAMP Postdoctoral Fellowship (2005–2008) at the Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics, where he began collaborating with Mikhail Lukin's group on Rydberg quantum optics.

His research centers on the theoretical description of strongly interacting Rydberg atoms and their applications to quantum nonlinear optics, many-body physics, and quantum information. He was a co-author on the landmark 2012 Nature paper (Peyronel et al.) demonstrating quantum nonlinear optics with single photons via Rydberg EIT, and contributed seminal theoretical work on photon-photon interactions via Rydberg blockade (Gorshkov et al., PRL 2011) and Rydberg crystallization (Pohl, Demler, Lukin, PRL 2010). His group at TU Wien continues to develop theories for quantum light manipulation in ordered atomic arrays and open quantum systems.
