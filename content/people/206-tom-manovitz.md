Dr. Tom Manovitz is an experimental physicist who joined the Department of Physics of Complex Systems at the Weizmann Institute of Science in October 2025, where he leads a new laboratory using high-resolution laser arrays to trap and manipulate individual neutral atoms as a platform for building and probing large, highly entangled quantum systems. His group focuses on entanglement and quantum many-body physics, hardware for neutral-atom quantum processors, and quantum computation and error correction.

Manovitz earned his MSc and PhD at the Weizmann Institute under Prof. Roee Ozeri, working on trapped-ion quantum computing with robust entangling gates and quantum coherent feedback. He then spent four years as a postdoctoral fellow in Prof. Mikhail Lukin's group at the Max Planck-Harvard Center for Quantum Optics, where he helped develop reconfigurable neutral-atom (Rydberg) arrays and contributed to several landmark *Nature* papers, including the 2024 demonstration of a logical quantum processor based on reconfigurable atom arrays.

His research bridges two architectures — from ions trapped in oscillating electric fields to neutral atoms held in optical tweezers — to explore complex quantum phenomena and advance quantum computing.
