Guido Pupillo is a Distinguished Professor at the Université de Strasbourg, where he directs the European Center for Quantum Sciences (CESQ) and the Quantum Physics Laboratory at ISIS. He is a leading theorist in the physics of Rydberg-dressed atoms and strongly correlated quantum many-body systems.

He received his PhD in 2005 from the University of Maryland, College Park, with research conducted at the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in Gaithersburg, under Carl Williams. His dissertation focused on confined ultracold bosons in one-dimensional optical lattices. From 2005 to 2011 he was a postdoctoral researcher and then senior scientist at the University of Innsbruck and the Austrian Academy of Sciences, working in the group of Peter Zoller, where he obtained his Habilitation in 2011. He joined the Université de Strasbourg as full Professor in 2012.

Pupillo's foundational contribution is the proposal to generate long-range tunable interactions between ground-state atoms by weakly admixing excited Rydberg states with laser light ("Rydberg dressing"), enabling the engineering of exotic many-body phases such as supersolids and cluster Luttinger liquids. He coordinates the Horizon Europe Quantum Flagship programme EuRyQa (European Infrastructure for Rydberg Quantum Computing) and the French national PEPR-Q programme aQCess. He holds two senior chairs from the Institut Universitaire de France (Fundamental Chair 2019, Innovation Chair 2025) and is co-founder of the quantum computing startup QPerfect.
