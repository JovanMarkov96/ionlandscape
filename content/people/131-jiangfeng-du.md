Jiangfeng Du (born June 1969, Wuxi, Jiangsu) is a Chinese physicist and academician of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (elected 2015) who built one of the world's leading groups in spin-based quantum information and precision measurement using nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond.

He entered USTC's class for gifted young people at age 16 in 1985, and earned his bachelor's, master's (1997), and PhD (2000) from the Department of Modern Physics at USTC. He joined the faculty immediately after graduation, became Associate Professor in 2000, Professor in 2004, and led the CAS Key Laboratory of Microscale Magnetic Resonance at Hefei National Laboratory for Physical Sciences at the Microscale. From 2005–2007 he conducted research at Dortmund University, Germany. He served as Vice President of USTC from April 2018, then as President of Zhejiang University from December 2022, and was appointed Vice Minister of Education of China in February 2025.

A major early result was his 2009 *Nature* paper demonstrating optimal dynamical decoupling to preserve electron spin coherence in solids — a technique now widely adopted in the NV-center community. His group subsequently achieved single-nuclear-spin cluster sensing at atomic-scale resolution (*Nature Physics* 2014), ambient-condition single-protein NMR spectroscopy (*Science* 2015), the first observation of parity-time symmetry breaking in a single-spin system (*Science* 2019), and entanglement-enhanced magnetometry beating the standard quantum limit (*Science Advances* 2021). He also founded CIQTEK in 2016, commercialising NV-center quantum sensing instruments.
