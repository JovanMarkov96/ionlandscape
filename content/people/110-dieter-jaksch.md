Dieter Jaksch is a theoretical physicist and Professor for Theory of Many-Body Quantum Optical Systems at the Institute for Quantum Physics, Universität Hamburg, which he joined in the winter semester of 2021/22. He was previously Professor of Physics at the University of Oxford (2010–2021), where he had been a faculty member since 2003.

He received his MSc (1996) and PhD in Natural Sciences (1999) from the University of Innsbruck, Austria, working in the quantum optics group closely associated with Peter Zoller. In 2001 he was awarded the "Promotio sub auspiciis Praesidentis rei publicae," Austria's highest doctoral distinction.

Jaksch is best known for co-proposing the use of cold bosonic atoms in optical lattices to realise the Bose-Hubbard model (1998, with Bruder, Cirac, Gardiner, and Zoller), a landmark paper that provided the theoretical basis for Greiner and Bloch's observation of the superfluid–Mott-insulator transition. In 2000 he co-proposed the Rydberg blockade mechanism for fast two-qubit quantum gates in neutral-atom arrays (with Cirac, Zoller, Rolston, Côté, and Lukin), which underpins the dominant gate approach in modern Rydberg qubit processors. He also co-proposed using laser-assisted tunnelling to create effective magnetic fields for neutral atoms in optical lattices (the Hofstadter butterfly, 2003). His current research focuses on non-equilibrium dynamics, tensor network methods, and variational quantum algorithms for nonlinear partial differential equations. He is a Fellow of the Institute of Physics and was awarded the Thomas Young Medal and Prize (2018) and an ERC Synergy Grant (2013).
