Nicolas Gisin (born 1952) is Professor Emeritus of Physics at the University of Geneva, where he led the Quantum Information and Communication (QIC) group in the Department of Applied Physics for more than two decades. He is one of the founding figures of experimental quantum cryptography and long-distance quantum communication. In 1993 he demonstrated quantum key distribution in optical fiber, and in 1997 his group performed the first Bell inequality violation test with photons more than 10 km apart over deployed telecom fiber beneath Lake Geneva. In 2003 the group achieved the first long-distance teleportation of qubits at telecommunication wavelengths. He co-founded ID Quantique in 2001, one of the world's first commercial quantum cryptography companies. His 2002 Reviews of Modern Physics article on quantum cryptography (with Ribordy, Tittel, and Zbinden) has accumulated more than 12,000 citations and remains the standard reference in the field. Despite emeritus status he remains research-active, publishing in 2024 on Bell inequalities and foundational quantum mechanics, and was awarded the Micius Quantum Prize in 2023 and an honorary doctorate from the University of York in 2025.
