Adam M. Kaufman is a JILA Fellow, NIST Physicist, and Associate Professor of Physics at the University of Colorado Boulder, where he leads the Kaufman Group at JILA. He joined JILA as an Associate Fellow and Assistant Professor Adjoint in July 2017 and was promoted to JILA Fellow and Associate Professor in March 2022.

He received his PhD in 2015 from the University of Colorado Boulder / JILA under the supervision of Cindy A. Regal, where he demonstrated ground-state cooling and quantum control of single neutral atoms in optical tweezers. He then completed a postdoctoral fellowship (2015–2017) in the Greiner Lab at Harvard University, contributing to landmark experiments on quantum thermalization and entanglement in many-body quantum systems.

Kaufman is a pioneer of optical tweezer array platforms for quantum computing and metrology, particularly with alkaline-earth (strontium) atoms. His 2018 demonstration of microscopic control of single strontium atoms in optical tweezers opened a new frontier for neutral-atom quantum information processing with atoms possessing rich internal structure. His group has since demonstrated seconds-scale coherence on an optical clock transition in tweezer arrays, long-lived entangled Bell states with optical clock qubits, and contributed broadly to establishing tweezer arrays as a leading quantum computing architecture.

He was awarded the 2023 Breakthrough New Horizons in Physics Prize "for the development of optical tweezer arrays to realize control of individual atoms for applications in quantum information science, metrology, and molecular physics," and also received the Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers (PECASE).
