Tobias Heindel is Professor of Photonic Quantum Networks at the Department for Quantum Technology, University of Münster. He is one of Germany's leading experimentalists in semiconductor quantum photonics, specialising in the development of high-purity quantum dot single-photon sources and their deployment in quantum key distribution (QKD) and photonic quantum networks.

Heindel studied physics at Julius-Maximilians-Universität Würzburg, receiving his Diploma in 2009. He remained at Würzburg for his doctoral work, supervised by Stephan Reitzenstein, completing a PhD in 2014 with the thesis "Elektrisch gepumpte Quantenpunkt-Einzelphotonenquellen für die Quantenkommunikation" (Electrically Pumped Quantum-Dot Single-Photon Sources for Quantum Communication). As part of this work he co-authored the first proof-of-principle QKD experiment using electrically driven semiconductor quantum dot sources (NJP, 2012; DOI: 10.1088/1367-2630/14/8/083001). From 2014 to 2018 he was a postdoctoral researcher in Reitzenstein's Optoelectronics and Quantum Devices group at TU Berlin, where he helped develop deterministic in situ electron-beam lithography techniques for quantum-dot microlenses and micropillars, producing photons with indistinguishability up to 80% (Nature Communications, 2015; DOI: 10.1038/ncomms8662).

In 2018 the German Federal Ministry for Education and Research (BMBF) awarded Heindel a €3.1 million Junior Research Group Grant (project QuSecure), enabling him to establish the Quantum Communication Systems group at the Institute of Solid State Physics of TU Berlin. In 2025 he was appointed full Professor of Photonic Quantum Networks at the University of Münster's Department for Quantum Technology, where he continues to lead the group under the same name. His major 2023 review in Advances in Optics and Photonics (DOI: 10.1364/AOP.490091, with Kim, Gregersen, Rastelli, and Reitzenstein) is the comprehensive reference covering quantum dot devices for photonic quantum information technology.

Awards include the Karl Scheel Prize of the Physical Society of Berlin (2020) and the Institute of Physics Lise Meitner Medal and Prize (2025) for the science-communication project QuanTour — a quantum single-photon source that has toured laboratories across Europe, documented via podcast and social media.
