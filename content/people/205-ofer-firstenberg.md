Prof. Ofer Firstenberg is an experimental physicist in the Department of Physics of Complex Systems at the Weizmann Institute of Science, where he leads the **Light-vs-Matter Lab**. His research asks whether individual photons can be made to behave like massive particles — and atoms like light — through strong, controllable photon-photon interactions. The group works on photon-photon interaction in cold Rydberg gases (photonic molecules and crystals), hot quantum memories spanning nanoseconds to hours, and atom interferometry.

Firstenberg earned a BSc in Physics at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem within the IDF Talpiot program, followed by an MSc and PhD at the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology, with doctoral research conducted at Rafael Advanced Defense Systems under the co-supervision of Amiram Ron (Technion) and Nir Davidson (Weizmann). He then carried out postdoctoral research at the **Harvard-MIT Center for Ultracold Atoms** in the groups of Mikhail Lukin and Vladan Vuletić, where he led experiments that coaxed photons into binding together as molecules — a landmark 2013 *Nature* result. He joined the Weizmann Institute faculty around 2014.

In 2026 he co-founded **Q-Factor**, an Israeli neutral-atom quantum-computing startup spun out of Weizmann and the Technion.
