Hannes Bernien is a Full Professor of Experimental Physics at the University of Innsbruck and Scientific Director at the Institute for Quantum Optics and Quantum Information (IQOQI) of the Austrian Academy of Sciences, positions he assumed in 2025. Prior to this, he was an Assistant Professor of Molecular Engineering at the University of Chicago's Pritzker School of Molecular Engineering (2019–2025).

Bernien received his Master's degree in physics from the University of Hanover, Germany, and his PhD (cum laude) in 2014 from Delft University of Technology under Ronald Hanson. During his doctoral work at the Kavli Institute of Nanoscience Delft, he pioneered experiments on quantum networking with nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond, including the first heralded entanglement between solid-state qubits over three metres (Nature, 2013) and contributing to the landmark loophole-free Bell inequality violation (Nature, 2015). He then did a postdoctoral fellowship (2015–2019) in Mikhail Lukin's group at Harvard, where he co-developed the Rydberg atom array platform and led the landmark 51-atom quantum simulator experiment (Nature, 2017).

Bernien's research combines techniques from quantum control, quantum optics, and ultracold atoms to develop large-scale quantum processors and quantum networks. His group pioneered dual-species Rydberg arrays (Rb and Cs) for quantum computing and networking, demonstrated mid-circuit error correction using spectator qubits, and is developing modular quantum network nodes coupling atom arrays to nanophotonic interfaces at telecom wavelengths.

He has received the 2023 New Horizons in Physics Prize (Breakthrough Foundation, shared with Endres, Kaufman, Thompson, Ni, and Pichler) for optical tweezer control of individual atoms, an NSF CAREER Award, a Sloan Research Fellowship in Physics (2021), and the International Quantum Technology Young Scientist Award (IOP, 2020).
