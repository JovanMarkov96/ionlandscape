Peter Zoller is a theoretical physicist and one of the founding figures of quantum information science. He is Emeritus Professor of Theoretical Physics at the University of Innsbruck and served as Scientific Director at the Institute for Quantum Optics and Quantum Information (IQOQI) of the Austrian Academy of Sciences from 2003 to 2024.

He received his PhD in 1977 from the University of Innsbruck under Fritz Ehlotzky, working on the Stark effect. He subsequently held postdoctoral positions with Peter Lambropoulos at the University of Southern California (1978–1979) and with Dan Walls at the University of Waikato (1980). From 1991 to 1994 he was a Professor at the University of Colorado, Boulder and JILA Fellow, before returning to Innsbruck in late 1994 as chair professor for theoretical quantum optics.

Zoller is best known for co-proposing, with Ignacio Cirac, the first concrete scheme for a quantum computer using cold trapped ions (the **Cirac–Zoller gate**, 1995) — the foundational proposal that launched experimental trapped-ion quantum computing and the laser-controlled ion-trap gate paradigm used by groups and companies worldwide. His work spans essentially every major thread of quantum information with atoms and light:

- **Trapped-ion quantum computing** — the Cirac–Zoller controlled-NOT gate and subsequent ion-trap proposals.
- **Quantum simulation and many-body physics** — the proposal (with Jaksch, Bruder, Cirac and Lewenstein, 1998) to realize the Bose–Hubbard model with cold bosonic atoms in optical lattices, seeding the field of optical-lattice quantum simulation; and a Rydberg quantum simulator (2010).
- **Neutral-atom and Rydberg gates** — the cold-controlled-collision gate (1999) and the Rydberg-blockade quantum gate (Jaksch, Cirac, Zoller, Côté and Lukin, 2000), the basis of today's neutral-atom/Rydberg quantum processors.
- **Quantum networks and repeaters** — proposals for atom–photon quantum networks (with Cirac, Kimble and Mabuchi, 1997) and quantum repeaters (with Briegel, Dür and Cirac, 1998).
- **Open quantum systems** — quantum-trajectory methods and dissipative (reservoir-engineered) state preparation and computation.

He served as Scientific Director of IQOQI (2003–2024) and in 2017 **co-founded Alpine Quantum Technologies (AQT)**, the University of Innsbruck spin-off commercializing trapped-ion quantum computers, together with Rainer Blatt and Thomas Monz. His honours include the Wolf Prize in Physics (2013, with Cirac), the Dirac Medal (2006), the Max Planck Medal (2005), the Benjamin Franklin Medal (2010), the BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award (2008, with Cirac) and the Micius Quantum Prize (2020).
