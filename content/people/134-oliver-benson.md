Oliver Benson is Professor of Experimental Nano-Optics at Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin, where he has led the Nano Optics group at the Institute of Physics since 2001. His research spans quantum optics, single-photon sources, NV-center photonics in diamond, nano-optics, cavity QED, and quantum plasmonics.

He completed his PhD (1995) at Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München in collaboration with the Max Planck Institute for Quantum Optics in Garching. He then moved to Stanford University as a Feodor Lynen Fellow of the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation (1997–1999), working in Yoshihisa Yamamoto's group at the Edward L. Ginzton Laboratory, where he contributed to landmark work on single-photon turnstile devices and entangled photon pairs from quantum dots. He subsequently led an Emmy-Noether junior research group at Universität Konstanz before joining Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin in 2001.

Benson's group is recognized for pioneering work coupling single NV centers in diamond nanocrystals to photonic crystal cavities, optical nanofibers, and hybrid metal-dielectric nanoantennas, achieving high single-photon collection efficiencies. His 2020 Nature Photonics review on hybrid integrated quantum photonic circuits has become a widely cited reference in the field.
