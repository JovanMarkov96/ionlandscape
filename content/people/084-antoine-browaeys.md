Antoine Browaeys is Directeur de recherche (Research Director) at the French National Centre for Scientific Research (CNRS), based at the Laboratoire Charles Fabry, Institut d'Optique Graduate School in Palaiseau, France. He is one of the world's foremost experimental physicists in the field of neutral atom quantum simulation and computing, pioneering the use of optical tweezer arrays loaded with individual Rydberg atoms.

He graduated from ENS Paris-Saclay (formerly ENS Cachan) and completed his PhD in 2000 at Laboratoire Charles Fabry under the supervision of Alain Aspect, focusing on magnetic trapping of metastable helium gas. Following his doctorate, he carried out postdoctoral research at the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) in the United States with William D. Phillips. He joined the CNRS in 2003 as Chargé de recherche and was promoted to Directeur de recherche in 2013.

At Institut d'Optique, Browaeys co-leads the "Quantum Optics – Atoms" group with Thierry Lahaye. His team's landmark contributions include the first experimental demonstration of Rydberg blockade between two individual atoms (2009), the first entanglement of two neutral atoms via Rydberg blockade (2010), defect-free atom-by-atom assembly of 2D atomic arrays (2016), and large-scale quantum simulation of frustrated magnets and topological phases with hundreds of atoms. In 2019, he co-founded Pasqal, a quantum computing company commercialising neutral-atom processors based on his group's technology.

Browaeys is a member of the French Academy of Sciences. He has received the CNRS Silver Medal (2021), the Aimé Cotton Prize from the French Physical Society, the Alfred-Verdaguer Prize from the French Academy of Sciences, and was named the 2026 Herbert Walther Award recipient (Optica / DPG).
