Nicholas Hutzler leads a group at the California Institute of Technology that develops cold and ultracold polyatomic molecules — such as calcium monohydroxide (CaOH) and ytterbium monohydroxide (YbOH) — as quantum sensors for precision searches of physics beyond the Standard Model, including the electron's electric dipole moment.

He earned his PhD at Harvard University in 2014 with John M. Doyle, where he was a central member of the ACME collaboration that used a thorium monoxide (ThO) beam to set a then-record limit on the electron EDM, and where he developed the cryogenic buffer-gas beam source now used worldwide. He joined the Caltech faculty in 2017.
