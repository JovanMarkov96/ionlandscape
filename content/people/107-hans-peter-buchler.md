Hans Peter Büchler is Professor of Theoretical Physics at the Institute for Theoretical Physics III (ITP3) at the University of Stuttgart, where he has held the chair since 2007. He holds the degree Dr. sc. nat. (Doctor of Natural Sciences) from ETH Zürich (2003), where he worked under Gianni Blatter on phase transitions in quantum condensed matter. He then carried out postdoctoral research from 2004 to 2007 with Peter Zoller at the University of Innsbruck, which shaped his focus on cold-atom quantum simulation and Rydberg physics.

His research centres on the theoretical description of quantum many-body systems, including cold quantum gases, Rydberg atoms, topological states of matter, and dipolar molecules. He co-authored the landmark proposal for a Rydberg quantum simulator in 2010 (with Weimer, Müller, Lesanovsky, and Zoller), which showed that laser-excited Rydberg atoms provide a universal platform for simulating exotic spin models including topological codes. His earlier work with Micheli and Zoller demonstrated that cold polar molecules can generate effective three-body interactions, an ingredient for engineering highly correlated quantum phases. His group later contributed to the first experimental observation of a symmetry-protected topological phase using Rydberg atoms in optical tweezers (Science 2019, with Browaeys and Lahaye groups). More recently he has worked on Rydberg polariton physics and schemes for fractional Chern insulators with Rydberg atom arrays.

In 2016 he received an ERC Consolidator Grant for his project SIRPOL (Strongly Interacting Rydberg Slow-Light Polaritons).
