Markus Greiner is the George Vasmer Leverett Professor of Physics at Harvard University, where he has been a faculty member since 2005. He is one of the principal architects of quantum gas microscopy — the technique that enables single-atom imaging and manipulation within optical lattices at the scale of individual lattice sites — and a pioneer of quantum simulation with ultracold atoms.

He received his PhD in 2003 from Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München under Nobel laureate Theodor W. Hänsch, where his doctoral work produced the landmark 2002 Nature paper (with Bloch, Mandel, Esslinger, and Hänsch) demonstrating the first quantum phase transition from a superfluid to a Mott insulator in a gas of ultracold atoms. Before joining Harvard he was a postdoctoral researcher (2003–2005) at JILA (University of Colorado Boulder / NIST) with Deborah Jin, where he realised a fermionic superfluid from ultracold atoms.

At Harvard, Greiner's group invented the quantum gas microscope (2009), enabling fluorescence imaging of individual rubidium atoms in a Hubbard-regime optical lattice. Subsequent milestones include site-resolved imaging of a fermionic Mott insulator (2016) and experimental observation of quantum thermalization through entanglement (2016). He is a MacArthur Fellow (2011), I.I. Rabi Prize recipient (2013), and Fellow of the American Physical Society (2017). He co-directs the Harvard–MIT Center for Ultracold Atoms and the Max Planck–Harvard Research Center for Quantum Optics.
