Mikael Afzelius is a Maître d'enseignement et de recherche (senior lecturer/MER) in the Department of Applied Physics at the University of Geneva, where he leads the qram (Quantum Repeaters and Memories) group. He is best known as a co-inventor of the atomic frequency comb (AFC) quantum memory protocol, introduced in a landmark 2009 Physical Review A paper with Christoph Simon, Hugues de Riedmatten, and Nicolas Gisin, which proposed storing light in an inhomogeneously broadened optical transition spectrally shaped into a periodic comb structure and has since become one of the most widely used protocols for rare-earth quantum memories.

In 2010 his group published the first demonstration of AFC memory with spin-wave storage — converting optical excitations to spin-wave excitations for on-demand retrieval — in a praseodymium-doped crystal. A 2008 Nature paper (with de Riedmatten, Staudt, Simon, and Gisin) demonstrated the first coherent reversible mapping of a sub-single-photon light field onto a solid-state crystal, establishing rare-earth-doped crystals as a viable platform. In 2011 (Clausen, Usmani, Bussières, Sangouard, Afzelius, de Riedmatten, Gisin, Nature 469) his group achieved quantum storage of photonic entanglement in a crystal with telecom-wavelength photons. In 2022 his team set a world record for solid-state qubit storage coherence, storing photonic time-bin qubits in a 151Eu3+:Y2SiO5 crystal for up to 20 ms with verified quantum fidelity, and extended spin coherence beyond 100 ms using dynamical decoupling.

His Google Scholar profile (retrieved 2026-05-30) records more than 11,600 citations and an h-index of 53 across approximately 145 publications. He is a long-term collaborator of Nicolas Gisin (182-nicolas-gisin) within the UNIGE Applied Physics quantum information and communication group.
