Klaus Mølmer is a theoretical physicist and one of the founding figures of quantum optics and quantum information theory. He is Professor of Physics at the Niels Bohr Institute, University of Copenhagen, where he joined in 2022 after holding a full professorship at Aarhus University from 2000 to 2022.

He received his Cand.scient. (Master's) degree from Aarhus University in 1987 and his PhD in 1990 under Knud Taulbjerg, initially working on atomic collision dynamics before pivoting to quantum optics. He subsequently spent an extended period at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris collaborating on laser cooling and quantum optics before returning to Aarhus as an Associate Professor (1991–2000) and then Full Professor (2000–2022).

Mølmer is co-inventor of the Mølmer–Sørensen gate, a two-qubit entangling gate for trapped ions that operates without requiring resolved sideband cooling, proposed in two landmark 1999 Physical Review Letters papers co-authored with Anders Sørensen. He is also one of the principal developers of the quantum jump / Monte Carlo wave-function method for simulating open quantum systems (1992–1993, with Jean Dalibard and Yvan Castin), and co-authored influential work on entanglement witnesses via spin squeezing (2001, with Sørensen). His research spans theoretical quantum optics, quantum measurement, quantum simulation, and quantum information.

He received the Humboldt Research Prize (2023) and the Carlsberg Foundation Research Prize (2025).
