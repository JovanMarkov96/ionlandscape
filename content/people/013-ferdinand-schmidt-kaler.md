Ferdinand Schmidt-Kaler is a Full Professor of Experimental Physics at Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz, where he leads the "Quantenbit AG" research group, and is a member of the Helmholtz Institute Mainz. 

His research focuses on trapped-ion quantum computing, particularly pioneering work in segmented-trap technologies, fast ion transport, scalable architectures, and improving coherence and gate performance. 

Prior to his appointment in Mainz in 2010, he was a Full Professor at the University of Ulm (2005–2010). He previously worked as a senior research assistant with Rainer Blatt at the University of Innsbruck (1995–2005), where he was a key contributor to the first realization of the Cirac-Zoller controlled-NOT quantum gate in 2003. He completed his PhD (1992) and an initial postdoc under Theodor W. Hänsch at LMU Munich/MPQ, followed by a postdoc with Serge Haroche at ENS Paris.
