Prof. Nir Davidson is an experimental physicist in the Department of Physics of Complex Systems at the Weizmann Institute of Science, where he leads a cold-atom and laser-physics laboratory. His research spans the dynamics and precision spectroscopy of ultracold atoms, quantum-degenerate Bose and Fermi gases, atom interferometry, coupled-laser networks, and slow and stored light in hot atomic vapor.

After doctoral work in optics at the Weizmann Institute, Davidson carried out postdoctoral research at Stanford University in the group of Nobel laureate Steven Chu, where he demonstrated "dark" blue-detuned optical dipole traps that confine atoms in a region of darkness, dramatically reducing perturbations of the atomic levels and enabling long atomic coherence times. He subsequently joined the faculty of the Weizmann Institute, where he later served as Dean of the Faculty of Physics. He trained a generation of Israeli atomic physicists, including Roee Ozeri and Ofer Firstenberg.

In 2026 he co-founded **Q-Factor**, an Israeli startup commercializing neutral-atom quantum-computing research from the Weizmann Institute and the Technion toward the million-qubit regime.
