Christopher R. Monroe is a physicist and one of the world's leading figures in trapped-ion quantum information science. He is the Gilhuly Family Presidential Distinguished Professor of Electrical & Computer Engineering and Physics at Duke University and the founding director of the Duke Quantum Center, and he co-founded IonQ, the first publicly traded pure-play quantum-computing company. As a postdoctoral fellow and staff scientist in David J. Wineland's Ion Storage Group at NIST (1992–2000), he was the first author on the demonstration of the first quantum logic gate (1995) and on the first "Schrödinger cat" superposition state of a single atom (1996); Wineland went on to share the 2012 Nobel Prize in Physics. Across his career at NIST, the University of Michigan, the University of Maryland (where he was a fellow of the Joint Quantum Institute) and Duke, his group has pioneered high-fidelity entangling gates, photonic networking of remote ions, programmable quantum simulators of 50+ spins, and modular quantum-computer architectures, working with 171Yb⁺, 9Be⁺, 111Cd⁺ and 138Ba⁺ ions. He is a member of the U.S. National Academy of Sciences and a recipient of the American Physical Society's Arthur Schawlow Prize for Laser Science and the I. I. Rabi Award.

## Career

- **Duke University** (2021–present): Gilhuly Family Presidential Distinguished Professor; Professor of Physics and of Electrical & Computer Engineering; Founding Director of the Duke Quantum Center. Also College Park Professor of Physics at the University of Maryland (2021–present).
- **IonQ, Inc.** (2016–2023): Co-founder and Chief Scientist; Chief Executive Officer (2018–2019). Co-founded with Jungsang Kim; IonQ became a public company in 2021.
- **University of Maryland, College Park** (2007–2021): Bice Zorn Professor of Physics; Fellow of the Joint Quantum Institute (JQI); Distinguished University Professor (2015–2021); Fellow of the Joint Center for Quantum Information and Computer Science (QuICS, 2014–2021).
- **University of Michigan, Ann Arbor** (2000–2007): Associate Professor (2000–2003) then Professor of Physics; Professor of Electrical Engineering & Computer Science and Director of the NSF FOCUS Center (2006–2007).
- **NIST, Boulder** (1992–2000): NRC Postdoctoral Researcher (1992–1994, mentor David J. Wineland) and then Staff Physicist and Project Leader (1994–2000) in the Ion Storage Group.
- **Education**: Ph.D. in Physics, University of Colorado Boulder, 1992 (advisor Carl Wieman); S.B. in Physics, MIT, 1987 (undergraduate research with Michael S. Feld).

## Landmark contributions

- **First quantum logic gate (1995)** — with Wineland at NIST, the first experimental demonstration of a universal (controlled-NOT) quantum logic gate, using a single 9Be⁺ ion.
- **Schrödinger-cat state of an atom (1996)** — creation of a mesoscopic superposition of a single trapped atom in two separated locations.
- **Ion-photon and remote-ion entanglement (2004–2007)** — entanglement between a single trapped atom and a single photon, and the first heralded entanglement of two ions separated by a meter, the building block for a quantum network.
- **Teleportation and device-independent randomness (2009–2010)** — quantum teleportation between distant matter qubits, and random numbers certified by a loophole-targeting Bell test.
- **Programmable quantum simulators and computers (2016–2017)** — small programmable general-purpose ion-trap quantum computers and quantum simulators of 50+ interacting spins, including the observation of a discrete time crystal and many-body dynamical phase transitions.
- **Modular architecture (2014)** — the large-scale modular quantum-computer architecture with atomic memory and photonic interconnects that underpins IonQ's approach.

## Ion species

Monroe's experiments have spanned several atomic ion species over his career: **9Be⁺** during his NIST years (the first logic gate and cat state), **111Cd⁺** at Michigan, and **171Yb⁺** — now his group's principal hyperfine qubit at Maryland and Duke. Recent dual-species quantum-networking nodes pair 171Yb⁺ memory qubits with **138Ba⁺** ions for efficient photon generation.
