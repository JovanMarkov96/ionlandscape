Hugues de Riedmatten is ICREA Professor and head of the Quantum Photonics with Solids and Atoms (QPSA) group at ICFO — The Institute of Photonic Sciences in Castelldefels, Spain, a position he has held since 2010. He is one of the world's leading experimentalists in quantum memories for light and quantum repeater hardware, with particular expertise in rare-earth-ion-doped crystals and cold atomic ensembles.

He studied Physics Engineering at EPFL and obtained his PhD in experimental quantum optics in 2003 from the University of Geneva under Nicolas Gisin, with a thesis on photonic entanglement in optical fibers. After a postdoc at Caltech in the group of H. Jeff Kimble (2003–2005), where he co-authored the landmark 2005 Nature paper demonstrating measurement-induced entanglement between remote atomic ensembles, he returned to Geneva as a senior scientist in the Gisin group, leading key experiments on solid-state quantum memories until 2010.

At ICFO his group has produced a series of landmark demonstrations. The 2008 Nature paper (de Riedmatten, Afzelius, Staudt, Simon, Gisin) was the first to coherently map a sub-single-photon field onto a solid-state atomic ensemble — a foundational result for solid-state quantum networks. With collaborators he co-authored the 2009 Physical Review A paper proposing the atomic frequency comb (AFC) memory protocol, which became the dominant framework for multimode solid-state quantum memories. His group demonstrated the first quantum storage of photonic entanglement in a crystal (Nature 2011), quantum correlations between telecom photons and an on-demand solid-state memory (Physical Review X 2017), and telecom-heralded entanglement between two remote multimode solid-state memories (Nature 2021) — a key milestone towards deployed quantum repeaters. He was awarded an ERC Starting Grant (QuLIMA, 2011–2016) and holds an ERC Advanced Grant and a Moore Foundation Frontier Research grant. He received the City of Barcelona Prize in 2018. His work has accumulated over 20,000 citations (h-index 65, Google Scholar).
