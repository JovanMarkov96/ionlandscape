Prof. Daniel Zajfman is an experimental physicist in the Department of Particle Physics and Astrophysics at the Weizmann Institute of Science, where his group ("Molecular Physics and Ion Trapping," with Dr. Oded Heber) studies the dynamics of single atomic, molecular, and cluster ions. He is a pioneer of the **electrostatic ion beam trap** — a linear "electrostatic bottle" that stores fast ion beams between facing electrostatic mirrors without magnetic fields — and uses such traps and storage rings to recreate the extreme conditions of interstellar space in the laboratory, probing dissociative recombination, internal decay processes, and the surprising self-bunching of trapped ions.

Born in Brussels in 1959, Zajfman earned his BSc (1983) and PhD (1989) in atomic physics at the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology, followed by postdoctoral research at Argonne National Laboratory (1989–1991). He joined the Weizmann Institute in 1991, becoming Associate Professor in 1997 and Full Professor in 2003. He has been an external member of the Max Planck Institute of Nuclear Physics in Heidelberg since 2001 and served as its Director in 2005–2006. From December 2006 to 2019 he served as the tenth **President of the Weizmann Institute of Science**.
