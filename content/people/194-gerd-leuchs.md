Gerd Leuchs (born 14 June 1950) is a German experimental physicist and Director Emeritus at the Max Planck Institute for the Science of Light (MPL) in Erlangen, Germany, and Senior Professor (emeritus) at Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg (FAU). He is one of the pioneering figures in continuous-variable (CV) quantum information and quantum optics, with foundational contributions to squeezed light, fiber-optical quantum communication, quantum key distribution, and polarization-structured light.

Leuchs studied physics and mathematics at the University of Cologne (1970–1975) and received his PhD from Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München in 1978 under Herbert Walther — a mentorship reflected decades later in his receipt of the 2018 Herbert Walther Prize. After a postdoc and faculty period at LMU Munich (1982–1994), he was appointed full professor of physics at FAU Erlangen-Nürnberg in 1994, a post he held until retirement in 2019. He was one of the founding directors of MPL when it was established in 2009, serving as director until 2019. He holds an adjunct professorship at the University of Ottawa and has been a foreign member of the Russian Academy of Sciences since 2016.

His early quantum optics work includes observations of photon antibunching (1979) and the first generation of squeezed light in second-harmonic generation (1990). In the 2000s his group at Erlangen became a world-leading centre for CV quantum information: generating continuous-variable EPR entanglement via fiber Kerr nonlinearity (PRL 2001) and demonstrating that CV-QKD can beat the 3 dB loss limit using squeezed-state encoding (PRL 2002). The parallel work on polarization squeezing and polarization entanglement (PRA 2002) opened a new sub-field of polarization quantum optics. His 2003 PRL paper on the sharper focus of a radially polarized beam (with Dorn and Quabis) has accumulated nearly 2,800 citations and remains the flagship result of his structured-light programme. His 2017 Philosophical Transactions review (with Khan, Elser, Dirmeier, and Marquardt) surveys the mature experimental landscape of coherent-state CV-QKD including telecom-device front ends and satellite links.

Notable alumni include Ulrik Lund Andersen (postdoc 2003–2006, now Professor and bigQ Director at the Technical University of Denmark) and Christoph Marquardt (long-term collaborator and MPL group leader). Leuchs served as President of Optica in 2024. Awards include the Quantum Electronics and Optics Prize of the European Physical Society (2005), the Herbert Walther Prize (2018, jointly OSA/DPG), the Bavarian Maximilian Order for Science and Art (2018), the Officer's Cross of the Order of Merit of the Federal Republic of Germany (2012), fellowship in the German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina (2005), and Fellow of the AAAS (2018).
