Lawrence Cheuk is an Assistant Professor of Physics at Princeton University, where his group builds optical-tweezer arrays of ultracold molecules — including calcium monofluoride (CaF) and sodium-cesium (NaCs) — for quantum simulation, quantum information and precision measurement.

He earned his PhD at MIT in 2017 with Martin Zwierlein, developing quantum-gas microscopy of fermionic atoms and studies of the Fermi-Hubbard model, then was a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard with John M. Doyle, where he worked on laser cooling and optical trapping of molecules. He joined Princeton in 2020.
