Jeff D. Thompson is an Associate Professor of Electrical and Computer Engineering at Princeton University, where he joined the faculty as an Assistant Professor in February 2016 and was promoted to Associate Professor in January 2022. He is also affiliated with the Princeton Quantum Initiative.

Thompson received his BS in Physics from Yale University in 2007, where he worked with Jack Harris on laser-cooling mechanical systems. He then spent a year working with ultracold atoms in Immanuel Bloch's group in Mainz, Germany, before beginning his PhD at Harvard University. He completed his PhD in Physics in 2014 under the supervision of Mikhail Lukin, with his thesis titled "A quantum interface between single atoms and nanophotonic structures," which included landmark demonstrations of coupling single atoms to nanoscale photonic crystal cavities. He then did a postdoctoral fellowship at MIT (2014–2016) before joining Princeton.

Thompson's research focuses on using individual neutral atoms—particularly ytterbium-171—trapped in optical tweezer arrays and driven through Rydberg states for quantum computing and quantum error correction. His group pioneered the use of alkaline-earth atoms (Yb) for neutral atom quantum computing, exploiting the metastable electronic structure to enable erasure conversion: a technique that transforms the dominant gate errors into detectable erasure errors, dramatically lowering the overhead for fault-tolerant quantum computing.

He received the 2023 Breakthrough New Horizons in Physics Prize (shared with five others) for the development of optical tweezers to control individual atoms, along with the Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers (PECASE), an NSF CAREER Award, and a Sloan Research Fellowship in Physics.
