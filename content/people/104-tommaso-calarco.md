Tommaso Calarco is Director of the Institute for Quantum Control (PGI-8) at Forschungszentrum Jülich, a position he has held since September 2018. He is concurrently Professor of Quantum Information at the University of Cologne and the University of Bologna. He is one of Europe's foremost figures in quantum technologies policy and quantum optimal control theory.

Born in 1969, he obtained his PhD from the University of Ferrara in 1998 and subsequently joined Peter Zoller's group at the University of Innsbruck as a postdoctoral researcher, where he co-developed early schemes for neutral-atom quantum gates using controlled collisions in optical traps. He later worked as a senior researcher at the BEC Centre in Trento, then as Professor and Director of the Institute for Complex Quantum Systems and the Centre for Integrated Quantum Science and Technology (IQST) at the University of Ulm, before moving to Jülich.

Calarco is a principal architect of optimal control methods for quantum technologies, particularly the application of chopped random-basis (CRAB) optimisation and related GRAPE/Krotov algorithms to quantum gate design, many-body dynamics, and open quantum systems. His 2000 Physical Review A paper with Hinds, Jaksch, Schmiedmayer, Cirac, and Zoller laid foundational groundwork for neutral-atom two-qubit gates via cold collisions. He co-led the European community quantum technologies roadmap (2018) and the strategic review of quantum optimal control (2022), both widely cited policy and review documents. He authored the Quantum Manifesto (2016) that provided the scientific basis for the European Commission's €1 billion Quantum Flagship initiative.
