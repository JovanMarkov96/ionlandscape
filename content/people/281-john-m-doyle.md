John M. Doyle is an experimental physicist at Harvard, the Henry B. Silsbee Professor of Physics and a co-director of the MIT-Harvard Center for Ultracold Atoms. He earned a BS in electrical engineering (1986) and a PhD in physics (1991) at MIT, advised by Daniel Kleppner and Thomas J. Greytak, with thesis work on the magnetic trapping of atomic hydrogen.

Doyle is a pioneer of buffer-gas cooling and the buffer-gas beam, general techniques for producing cold and ultracold molecules. His group demonstrated millikelvin magnetic trapping of calcium monohydride (CaH), and went on to laser-cool and trap diatomic molecules such as calcium monofluoride (CaF) and an expanding family of polyatomic radicals — calcium monohydroxide (CaOH), strontium monohydroxide (SrOH) and ytterbium monohydroxide (YbOH) — for quantum science and precision tests of fundamental physics, including collaboration on the ACME electron-EDM measurement using thorium monoxide (ThO). He received the 2021 Broida Prize and the 2024 Norman F. Ramsey Prize, was elected to the National Academy of Sciences, and served as APS President in 2025.
