QuEra Computing is a Boston-based quantum-computing company building hardware from
arrays of individually trapped, individually controlled **neutral rubidium atoms**.
Atoms are held in reconfigurable optical-tweezer arrays and entangled by exciting
them to high-lying **Rydberg states**, giving strong, programmable interactions.
The architecture supports both an **analog mode** — directly emulating quantum
many-body Hamiltonians — and a **digital, gate-based mode**, and it can physically
shuttle atoms to achieve long-range, all-to-all connectivity.

The company spun out of Harvard University and MIT in 2018, founded by Mikhail Lukin,
Markus Greiner, Vladan Vuletić, Dirk Englund, and Nathan Gemelke. It is led by CEO
Andy Ory, with co-founders Vladan Vuletić (CTO) and Mikhail Lukin (Chief Scientist)
continuing in technical leadership. Its flagship machine, **Aquila**, is a 256-qubit
processor available publicly through Amazon Braket, and the open-source **Bloqade**
SDK lets users design and simulate neutral-atom programs.

QuEra has been an early leader in **atom-array quantum error correction**: in 2023 it
co-authored the landmark demonstration of a logical quantum processor with up to 48
logical qubits on reconfigurable atom arrays. Its public roadmap targets a 30-logical-
qubit machine and, by 2026, a 100-logical-qubit system with more than 10,000 physical
qubits aimed at running deep logical circuits beyond the reach of classical simulation.
In 2025 the company raised a $230M+ financing round led by Google, later expanded with
participation from NVIDIA's NVentures.
