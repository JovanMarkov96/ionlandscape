Delft Circuits is a Delft, Netherlands company founded in 2016 by Sal Bosman (CTO) and Daan Kuitenbrouwer that tackles one of the least glamorous but most important bottlenecks in scaling quantum computers: the wiring. Superconducting and spin quantum processors sit inside dilution refrigerators, and each qubit traditionally needs bulky coaxial cables that take up space and dump heat into the cryostat, limiting how many qubits can be controlled.

Delft Circuits' answer is Cri/oFlex, a flexible superconducting cabling system that integrates many microwave control channels onto a thin, flexible substrate with low heat load and built-in microwave components. This lets far more control lines enter a fridge in the same space, which the company frames in a published I/O roadmap as a path toward systems with thousands of qubits.

The company has raised around EUR 15 million to scale Cri/oFlex, with backers including DeepTech XL, High-Tech Gründerfonds, QuVest, Paeonia Group and Waves Capital Partners, and in December 2025 it appointed Martin Danoesastro as CEO alongside an extension of its Series A.
