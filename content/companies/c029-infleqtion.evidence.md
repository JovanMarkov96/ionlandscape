## founding
- **source**: Source 1 (Wikipedia), Source 3 (TechCrunch)
- **note**: Founded approximately 2007 (some sources say 2007, Crunchbase lists 2007; Dana Anderson's JILA/CU Boulder group was the origin). Early products were BEC/cold-atom hardware for US government (DoD, DARPA, NIST) and academic customers. Mark Saffman (090-mark-saffman in our DB) did his PhD under Dana Anderson — the intellectual lineage is clear, but Saffman is not listed as a founder of ColdQuanta/Infleqtion.

## rebrand_note
- **source**: Source 2 (BusinessWire rebrand PR)
- **note**: Officially rebranded from ColdQuanta to Infleqtion on September 7, 2022. The rebrand coincided with the pivot from a pure cold-atom hardware supplier to a full-stack quantum technology company (computing + sensing + hardware). "ColdQuanta" is retained as an alias in this profile.

## funding_note
- **source**: Sources 3, 7 (TechCrunch, Crunchbase)
- **note**: Total raised ~$159M per Crunchbase by mid-2023. The $110M Series B (August 2021) is the most robustly documented round. Early rounds (Seed, Series A ~2018) have undisclosed amounts. A Series C of ~$49M is reported by Crunchbase but details are sparse. The $110M Baidu-led round is confirmed by multiple sources.

## technology
- **source**: Sources 4, 5 (Hilbert and Oqtant product pages)
- **note**: Hilbert uses Rydberg blockade-mediated two-qubit gates with rubidium atoms held in optical tweezers — the same physics as QuEra and Atom Computing but with different atom choice and control stack. Oqtant is distinct: it's a quantum matter/simulation platform (BEC in optical lattice) rather than a gate-based computer. Cold-atom module product line (dating to ColdQuanta era) continues to be sold for sensing, navigation, and timing applications.
