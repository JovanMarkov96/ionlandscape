Aquark Technologies is a Southampton, UK company founded in 2020 as a spin-out of the University of Southampton by Dr Andrei Dragomir (CEO) and Dr Alexander Jantzen, both former postgraduate researchers in the university's Quantum, Light and Matter group. It develops compact, robust cold-atom systems to bring quantum sensing, timing and computing out of the laboratory.

Cold atoms — clouds of atoms laser-cooled to near absolute zero — underpin the most precise atomic clocks and quantum sensors, but the apparatus to produce them has traditionally been large and delicate. Aquark's key innovation is a patented room-temperature cold-atom source, its "cold atom Cube," which shrinks the cold-atom chamber by roughly a factor of 100 into a portable, plug-and-play module, simplifying a complex technology so it can be deployed in the field for navigation, timing and sensing.

Aquark raised a EUR 5 million seed round led by the NATO Innovation Fund — the alliance's first investment in a quantum-technology company — and won a GBP 3.4 million Innovate UK contract to make its technology commercially ready, alongside trials with the Royal Navy.
