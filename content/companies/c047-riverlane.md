Riverlane is a Cambridge, UK company founded in 2016 by Dr Steve Brierley that is building the quantum error-correction (QEC) stack required to make quantum computers reliable. Quantum bits are intrinsically noisy, and without error correction a computation collapses after only a few operations; Riverlane focuses squarely on this bottleneck, which it frames as the key to moving from today's noisy machines to genuinely useful, fault-tolerant quantum computing.

The company's technology combines software with dedicated decoder hardware — its Deltaflow / QEC stack — that detects and corrects errors in real time, at the very high speeds quantum hardware demands. Crucially, it is designed to be hardware-agnostic, integrating with superconducting, trapped-ion and other qubit platforms rather than tying Riverlane to one type of machine. The company also publishes an annual Quantum Error Correction Report that tracks progress toward the millions of reliable quantum operations (QuOps) needed for practical applications.

Riverlane has raised significant capital to pursue this, including a GBP 15 million (~$18.7 million) Series B in 2023 led by Molten Ventures — with Altair, Cambridge Innovation Capital, Amadeus Capital Partners and the National Security Strategic Investment Fund — and a subsequent Series C of around $75 million to advance its real-time error-correction decoder chip.
