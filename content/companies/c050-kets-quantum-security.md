KETS Quantum Security is a Bristol, UK company founded in 2016 as a spin-out of the University of Bristol, building quantum-safe security hardware on integrated-photonic chips. Its founders — Chris Erven (CEO), Philip Sibson (CTO), Caroline Clark and Jake Kennard — come from Bristol's pioneering integrated-quantum-photonics group.

The company's central idea is to miniaturise the two key primitives of quantum security — quantum key distribution (QKD), which exchanges encryption keys with security guaranteed by physics, and quantum random number generation (QRNG), which produces unpredictable cryptographic randomness — onto small, low-power photonic chips. This chip-scale approach makes the technology suitable for demanding deployments such as telecom networks, defence systems and even drones, where size, weight and power are tightly constrained. KETS has demonstrated chip-based QKD with partners including Airbus and ID Quantique, for example to secure unmanned aerial vehicles.

KETS has raised around GBP 9.7 million in R&D grants and equity since its founding, including a GBP 3.1 million pre-Series A round backed by Quantonation, SpeedInvest and Mustard Seed MAZE, supported throughout by Innovate UK and the UK National Quantum Technologies Programme.
