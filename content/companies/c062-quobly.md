Quobly is a Grenoble, France quantum-computing company building a large-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computer based on silicon spin qubits. Founded in 2022 as Siquance and later renamed Quobly, it is the result of roughly fifteen years of joint research between CEA-Leti and CNRS, two of Europe's leading microelectronics and physics research organisations, and is led by co-founder and CEO Maud Vinet alongside co-founders Tristan Meunier and François Perruchot.

The company's central bet is that quantum computing should be built on the same manufacturing base as classical microelectronics. Quobly's quantum chips are co-designed with STMicroelectronics and fabricated on ST's industrial 300mm semiconductor platform, giving the company access to industrial-grade processes, reproducibility and a credible path to the very high qubit counts required for fault tolerance. It designs rack-scale quantum computers intended to drop into existing data centres, delivered across three product generations with modular upgrades under its Alloy product line.

Quobly raised approximately $23 million in 2025 to launch industrial production of a 100-qubit silicon chip, and in June 2026 closed a EUR 115 million (~$133.5 million) Series A led by STMicroelectronics, with Bpifrance, SEALSQ and Isalt, to industrialise its technology. With operations headquartered in Grenoble and additional offices in Singapore and Canada, it aims to deploy its first commercial quantum computer in the cloud by the end of 2026.
