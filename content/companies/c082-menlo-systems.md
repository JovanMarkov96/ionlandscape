Menlo Systems is a Munich-area (Martinsried), Germany photonics company founded in 2001 as a spin-off of the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics. It was co-founded by Professor Theodor W. Hänsch — who won the 2005 Nobel Prize in Physics for the optical frequency-comb technique — together with Ronald Holzwarth (CTO) and Michael Mei, and it pioneered the commercialisation of the optical frequency comb.

An optical frequency comb acts as a precise "ruler for light," letting scientists measure optical frequencies with extraordinary accuracy and compare and synchronise optical clocks. Menlo Systems supplies these combs along with ultrastable, ultra-low-noise lasers and synchronisation systems, which are foundational tools for optical atomic clocks, precision metrology, fundamental-physics tests and a range of quantum technologies.

With subsidiaries in the United States, Japan and China and a global distributor network, Menlo Systems remains closely connected to its customers in science and industry, and is one of Europe's key suppliers of the precision-photonics infrastructure that quantum and metrology research rely on.
