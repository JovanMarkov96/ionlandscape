C12 Quantum Electronics is a Paris company founded in 2020 by twin brothers Pierre (CEO) and Matthieu (CTO) Desjardins, spun out of the physics laboratory of the École Normale Supérieure. It is pursuing a distinctive approach to quantum hardware: building spin qubits from electrons hosted in suspended carbon nanotubes.

Carbon nanotubes can be grown to extraordinary purity, and C12's thesis is that hosting qubits in this ultra-clean material yields long coherence times and low error rates, addressing the fidelity problems that limit many qubit technologies. By integrating the nanotubes onto industrial silicon chips, the company aims for a CMOS-compatible, scalable route toward a large-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computer. To control its own manufacturing, C12 has built a "Quantum Fab" pilot line in Paris, inaugurated in 2023, allowing it to produce its quantum devices in-house.

The company raised an initial $10 million seed round led by 360 Capital in 2021 and subsequently a EUR 18 million (~$19.4 million) pre-Series A round, supported by France's national quantum programme, to advance its carbon-nanotube quantum processors.
