QphoX is a Delft, Netherlands company founded in 2020 as a TU Delft spin-out by Simon Gröblacher, Frederik Hijazi (CEO) and Robert Stockill. It develops quantum transducers — devices it markets as a "Quantum Modem" — and is one of the first companies to take quantum transduction out of the university lab and toward a product.

The problem QphoX addresses is that superconducting quantum computers operate with microwave-frequency signals, which cannot travel far over cables without losing their fragile quantum information. QphoX's transducer converts those microwave signals into optical photons at telecom wavelengths by coupling the two through a mechanical resonator, allowing low-loss, high-fidelity transmission over optical fibre. This enables both the optical readout of qubits and, more ambitiously, the linking of separate quantum processors into distributed machines and, ultimately, a quantum internet.

The company raised a EUR 2 million seed round led by Quantonation in 2021, followed by a EUR 8 million round led by QDNL Participations together with a EUR 6.5 million EIC Accelerator grant, and in 2026 launched a quantum transducer product enabling distributed quantum computing over long-distance optical networks.
