Orange Quantum Systems (OrangeQS) is a Delft, Netherlands company founded in 2020 as a spin-out of TNO and QuTech. Its founders helped build Quantum Inspire — Europe's first publicly accessible quantum computer — and in doing so experienced first-hand how big a bottleneck it is to test a new quantum chip.

OrangeQS builds automated test-and-diagnostics systems that characterise quantum processors quickly and at scale, turning a slow, manual process into something closer to industrial chip testing. Its OrangeQS MAX system is designed to characterise quantum chips with 100 or more qubits, and its Quantum Diagnostics Libraries — proprietary software for automated qubit testing — can be licensed separately from the hardware. Its technical advisors include the quantum-information pioneer David DiVincenzo.

After bootstrapping commercially for its first years and raising a EUR 1.5 million pre-seed led by Cottonwood Technology Fund in 2023, the company extended its seed round to EUR 15 million (around $17.6 million), led by the European Innovation Council Fund, to accelerate the OrangeQS MAX product line and launch a partnership programme.
