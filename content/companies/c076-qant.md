Q.ANT is a Stuttgart, Germany deep-tech company founded in 2018 by Michael Förtsch as a spin-off of the laser-technology group TRUMPF. It develops light-based — photonic — data processing, and operates at the intersection of next-generation computing and quantum technology.

The company's core product is a photonic processor built on its own thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) chip platform, using an architecture it calls Light Empowered Native Arithmetics (LENA) to perform analog computation with photons rather than electrons. Q.ANT positions this as an answer to the energy crisis in AI and high-performance computing, citing test results promising large gains in energy efficiency and performance without complex active cooling, and it ships Native Processing Servers to selected partners. It runs its own TFLN chip pilot line in collaboration with the Institute for Microelectronics Stuttgart (IMS CHIPS), and applies the same photonic-integration expertise to quantum-sensing technology.

In July 2025 Q.ANT raised a EUR 62 million (~$72 million) Series A — described as the largest photonic-computing round in Europe — co-led by Cherry Ventures, UVC Partners and imec.xpand, with participation from TRUMPF and other deep-tech investors.
