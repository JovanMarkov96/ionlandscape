  differentiators:
  - Room-temperature, rack-mounted operation using standard telecom-grade optical
    fibre components — no cryogenic cooling or vacuum required, enabling data-centre-ready
    deployment with a 19-inch rack form factor
  - Proprietary quantum memory technology (time-bin multiplexed photonic modes in
    quantum memories) invented at the University of Oxford NQIT Hub, enabling programmable
    boson sampling without the per-photon hardware overhead typical of linear-optical
    approaches
  - PT-2 (launched Oct 2024) uses deterministic single-photon sources (Sparrow Quantum
    chips) and delivers ~40 qumodes at industry-relevant clock cycles; estimated 4,000x
    more powerful than PT-1; first photonic system to combine multiple photon sources
    in one unit
  - First quantum computer sold to a national defence department — UK Ministry of
    Defence PT-1 deployment (June 2022); by early 2025 ORCA had delivered 10 on-premises
    PT-series systems globally
  - Deep integration with NVIDIA CUDA-Q and NVQLink (Oct 2025) enabling tightly coupled
    hybrid quantum-classical workflows within standard HPC/GPU data centre infrastructure
  elevator_pitch: ORCA Computing (founded 2019, London; spun out of Oxford) builds
    room-temperature, rack-mounted photonic quantum computers based on time-bin multiplexed
    quantum memories. Its PT Series hardware plugs into standard data centres and HPC
    clusters alongside NVIDIA GPUs, targeting near-term hybrid quantum-AI workloads
    in generative ML, optimisation, and molecular simulation. The PT-1 was the first
    quantum computer sold to a national defence organisation (UK MoD, 2022); the PT-2
    (2024) delivers ~40 qumodes with deterministic single-photon sources and has been
    deployed at the UK National Quantum Computing Centre (2025). ORCA led the £11.6M
    UK government Quantum Data Centre of the Future consortium and has raised ~$19M
    in VC funding plus substantial Innovate UK grants.
